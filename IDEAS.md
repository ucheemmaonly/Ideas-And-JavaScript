### Part 1: Five Project Ideas

### Idea 1: Nigerian Food and Calorie Tracker

Source: A discussion in Reddit’s r/loseit community about the difficulty of tracking calories manually, alongside FAO food-composition information for Nigeria and the NaijaFit app.

The problem, in one sentence: People who want to monitor their calorie intake often find it difficult to record every meal manually and estimate the calories in Nigerian dishes.

Who has it: Students, gym beginners, office workers, and people who want to understand their eating habits.

How they cope today: They search for similar foods in existing apps, estimate portion sizes, write down their meals, or stop tracking because the process takes too much time.

My possible solution: I would build a web application where users can upload a picture of their food, select the Nigerian dish and portion size, and receive an estimated calorie count. Users could save meals in a daily food diary and track their estimated calorie intake throughout the day.

### Idea 2: Customer Measurement Manager

Source: A TechCabal feature about fashion technology and a LinkedIn build-in-public post about digital tools for Nigerian tailors and fashion designers.

The problem, in one sentence: Tailors can lose customer measurements when paper notebooks are misplaced, resulting in delays, repeated measurements, and costly mistakes.

Who has it: Bespoke tailors, fashion designers, and small tailoring businesses that serve repeat customers.

How they cope today: They record measurements in notebooks, search through old WhatsApp conversations, or ask returning customers to come in for measurements again.

My possible solution: I would build a web application where tailors can save customer names, phone numbers, body measurements, and outfit photos. They could search for a customer and retrieve their measurements whenever they return.

### Idea 3: Small Shop Inventory Tracker

Source: A Reddit discussion in the r/smallbusiness community about the challenges of tracking retail inventory, alongside research into Nigerian inventory-management services.

The problem, in one sentence: Small shop owners can lose sales and money because they do not know which products are running low or how much stock remains.

Who has it: Owners of provision shops, boutiques, cosmetic stores, mini-marts, and other small retail businesses.

How they cope today: They count products manually, write sales in notebooks, inspect shelves, or rely on memory to decide when to restock.

My possible solution: I would build a mobile-friendly web application where shop owners can add products, record sales, update stock quantities, and receive low-stock alerts. This would help them know when to restock before products run out.

### Idea 4: AbaMarket Watch — Local Market Price Comparison

Source: TechCabal's reporting on Nigerian food-price information and the Skaana platform, which provides product and market price information.

The problem, in one sentence: Shoppers can struggle to know where to buy food and household essentials at better prices because prices vary across markets and change frequently.

Who has it: Students, families, traders, and other people shopping on a limited budget in Aba.

How they cope today: They visit different markets, ask sellers for prices, contact friends, or rely on previous shopping experiences to estimate costs.

My possible solution: I would create a web application where users can compare recently reported prices for items such as rice, garri, tomatoes, and cooking oil across selected markets in Aba. Users could submit prices alongside the market name and date so shoppers can compare prices using recent information.

### Idea 5: WhatsApp Order Manager

Source: SPEEDFI's WhatsApp commerce tools, which describe challenges businesses face when managing orders and payment checks across conversations.

The problem, in one sentence: Small businesses that receive orders through WhatsApp can lose track of customer orders, payment status, and deliveries because information is scattered across chats.

Who has it: Fashion vendors, food sellers, hair vendors, and other small businesses that receive orders through WhatsApp.

How they cope today: They search old chats, check payment screenshots, write order details in notebooks, and contact customers individually to confirm payments and deliveries.

My possible solution: I would build an order-management dashboard where sellers can record customers, ordered items, prices, payment status, and delivery status. Each order would have its own record, making it easier to manage orders without searching through multiple conversations.

### Part 2: The Idea I Would Most Like to Build

### My Chosen Idea: Nigerian Food and Calorie Tracker

I would choose FoodSnap over the other four ideas because it addresses an everyday activity: eating. People who want to understand their eating habits may find it difficult to record every meal manually, especially when eating Nigerian dishes that are not always easy to identify in international calorie-tracking apps.

I also like this idea because it would allow me to practise JavaScript, React, file uploads, form handling, state management, and database operations. I could test it with students and other people who want to monitor their meals and use their feedback to improve the application.

The application would focus on Nigerian foods such as jollof rice, beans, yam, moi-moi, and eba with soup. Its calorie figures would be estimates, because a food photograph cannot reliably reveal the exact portion size, ingredients, or cooking oil used.

### The Smallest First Version I Could Build in Two Weeks

1\. Upload a food picture

Users can take a picture of their meal or upload an existing image.

2\. Select the food and portion

Users select or confirm the Nigerian dish and choose an approximate serving size.

3\. Estimate calories

The application displays an approximate calorie count using a small food database.

4\. Save meals in a diary

Users can record breakfast, lunch, dinner, and snacks.

5\. View daily totals

A dashboard shows the meals recorded and the estimated total calories for the day.

During the first two weeks, I would focus on making these features work on mobile and desktop. I would start with a predefined list of Nigerian foods rather than attempting to build a complex AI food-recognition system immediately. Automatic image recognition and advanced nutrition analysis could be added later.

My goal would be to test the first version with at least one person who tracks their food intake and use their feedback to improve it.

### One Real Person I Could Show It to for Feedback

I would show the first version to friends and relations who are interested in fitness, healthy eating, or tracking their meals.

I would ask them to record several meals, including food purchased from a local vendor, and explain whether the process is easy to use. I would also ask whether the estimated calorie counts are helpful, which features are confusing, and what would encourage them to use the application regularly.


Final choice: Calorie tracker is my preferred idea because it combines a practical everyday problem. Its first version is achievable within two weeks, and I can gather feedback directly from users before expanding its features.
